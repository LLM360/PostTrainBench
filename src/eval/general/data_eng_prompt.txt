We want to train the small LLM \`{model}\` to excel at {benchmark}.
Your job is **data engineering only**. The training recipe is fixed and locked.
Your only variable is the dataset you construct.

{benchmark} is a REASONING benchmark. The official scorer runs
\`multiple_choice(cot=True)\` and parses the final \`ANSWER: <LETTER>\` line that
appears AFTER free-form step-by-step reasoning, with \`max_tokens=16000\`.
Reasoning is REWARDED and needs token room. This single fact drives the whole
strategy below: your headline lever is **distilling the teacher's reasoning**
into your training data, NOT collecting answer-only rows.

**THINKING-ONLY FORMAT (target shape for every assistant turn).** Every training
row's assistant target is a single \`<think>...</think>\` reasoning block then exactly
one final \`ANSWER: <LETTER>\` line — literally
\`<think>\n{reasoning}\n</think>\n\nANSWER: X\`. The scorer takes the FIRST
\`^ANSWER:\` match over the full completion, so the \`ANSWER:\` line MUST come AFTER
\`</think>\` and must NOT appear anywhere inside the \`<think>\` block (a stray in-think
\`ANSWER:\` gets scored instead and mis-grades the row — we scrub it upstream AND
hard-gate it in the audit; see step 5).

## What "data engineering only" means here
- You DO NOT modify training code, hyperparameters, or model architecture.
- You DO build, mine, synthesize, filter, and dedupe the training dataset.
- A locked SFT+LoRA script (\`train_sft.py\`) consumes your dataset and produces \`final_model/\`.

## Objective
Construct one or more training datasets, audit each, train with the locked recipe,
and self-evaluate each candidate. Store your best model in the folder \`final_model\`
(copy — not symlink — the chosen experiment's \`final_model/\` into place; see step 10).

Treat the run as a chain of falsifiable experiments — each iteration must build on a
named predecessor. 10 experiments is a floor, not a target. The SLURM timer decides
when the run ends, not you (see "Operating discipline" near the end); keep iterating
until it kills your process.

## DATA STRATEGY — distill reasoning, don't collect answers

Your PRIMARY lever is **reasoning distillation**. Every training row's assistant
turn is THINKING-ONLY: a single \`<think>...</think>\` reasoning block then exactly
one final line \`ANSWER: <LETTER>\` (LETTER in A-D). That matches what the eval
rewards. Three HARD INVARIANTS per row (the audit hard-fails on a violation, step 5):
(1) EXACTLY ONE \`ANSWER: <LETTER>\` line; (2) it comes AFTER \`</think>\`; (3) NO
\`ANSWER:\` marker inside \`<think>\`. Keep the literal \`ANSWER:\` marker verbatim — do
NOT rename it (renaming to \`answer line:\` collapsed exp_016 to ~0.13).

**WARNING — answer-only data caps a reasoning benchmark.** If you train on rows
whose assistant turn is just \`ANSWER: C\` (or just the letter, or a one-line
justification), you teach the model to skip the reasoning the scorer rewards and
you will plateau low. The V3 runs lost a lot of score this way. Don't repeat it.

The build flow is **DISCOVER -> JUDGE -> SYNTHESIZE**:

1. **DISCOVER** live data on the HF Hub. Do NOT pick from a fixed hardcoded list.
   Explore what's actually trending / most-downloaded / most-liked right now and
   go WIDE — try sources you (and per \`$SHARED_LOG_CSV\`) have not used yet.
   \`\`\`bash
   # trending graduate-science MCQ datasets, ranked, with 1-row samples, to stdout:
   python3 discover_datasets.py
   # most-downloaded QA datasets, written as a compact digest you read in one shot:
   python3 discover_datasets.py --keywords "graduate physics chemistry biology molecular genetics MCQ" \
     --sort downloads --task-filter question-answering --limit 30 --out candidates_digest.json
   # most-liked, faster listing-only pass (skips per-dataset sample probing):
   python3 discover_datasets.py --sort likes --limit 20 --no-sample
   \`\`\`
   It auto-EXCLUDES any candidate whose id/tags/card mentions \`gpqa\`
   (contamination guard) and logs each exclusion to stderr. Read the digest, pick
   the candidates that look like graduate-level STEM MCQ, and load the question
   rows you want with the \`datasets\` library.

   **GO WIDE — now ENFORCED.** discover_datasets.py keeps a query log
   (\`--query-log\`, default \`experiments/discovery_query_log.json\`) and REFUSES
   (exit code 6) any \`--keywords\` whose normalized token set is too similar
   (Jaccard > \`--max-query-similarity\`, default 0.6) to a query it already accepted.
   If it refuses, it prints orthogonal axes and alternate sorts to try — VARY the
   search, don't re-run the same one. A prior run wasted its budget re-querying ~8
   near-duplicates and adopted only ~3% of what it surfaced, ignoring strong
   top-ranked sets (e.g. ceval/ceval-exam). Don't repeat that. Concretely:
   - Rotate the SUBJECT axis: astronomy, materials science, electrical/computer
     engineering, organic chemistry, genetics — not just "physics chemistry biology".
   - Rotate the FRAMING axis: non-MCQ sources you reformat into MCQ are fair game.
   - Rotate \`--sort\` (trending / downloads / likes) so you don't keep seeing the
     same head datasets.
   - Then ACTUALLY ADOPT the strong surfaced candidates. Surfacing a top-ranked
     dataset and then not loading it is the failure mode — feed promising ones
     through JUDGE and SYNTHESIZE. \`--force\` exists to override the gate, but only
     reach for it when you truly intend a deliberate re-run, not to dodge going wide.
   This reinforces (does not replace) the source-novelty cadence in Rules: every
   5th experiment still needs a brand-new \`--data-sources\` token.

2. **JUDGE** relevance/quality with the teacher as an LLM-as-judge — USE THIS. A
   deterministic source/topic list cannot tell a hard graduate-level MCQ from an
   easy or off-topic one; only the judge can. One tiny call per row decides
   keep/reject, parallelized, so this is cheap. Run it on mined candidates to
   filter for relevance AND difficulty BEFORE you distill:
   \`\`\`bash
   python3 teacher_judge.py --in candidates.jsonl --out verdicts.jsonl \
     --batch 50 --max-workers 8 --filter-out-rejected \
     --judge-max-tokens 3072 --max-unparseable-frac 0.4 \
     [--criterion "graduate-level STEM multiple-choice suitable for GPQA-style training"]
   # -> verdicts.jsonl.kept.jsonl has only kept rows, _judge_* keys stripped,
   #    schema-clean so it pipes straight into the next step.
   \`\`\`
   The teacher emits a long \`<think>...</think>\` preamble before its JSON verdict.
   The judge now strips that preamble and budgets \`--judge-max-tokens 3072\` (default)
   so the JSON survives — the old 120-token budget made every verdict default to
   keep=false and was abandoned. \`--max-unparseable-frac\` (default 0.4) is a safety
   gate: if MORE than that fraction of replies still fail to parse (rows tagged
   \`_judge_reason=__parse_failure__\`), teacher_judge.py EXITS 2 rather than silently
   dropping everything — raise \`--judge-max-tokens\` and retry if you hit it. Don't
   skip judging just because it once kept 0/1200; with these flags it works.

3. **SYNTHESIZE / DISTILL** with the teacher. This is where reasoning gets added.
   teacher_synth.py now emits the THINKING-ONLY shape
   (\`<think>\n{reasoning}\n</think>\n\nANSWER: X\`) by DEFAULT for BOTH rationale and
   generate modes — one closed \`<think>\` block then exactly one trailing \`ANSWER:\`
   line, none inside the block. \`--max-tokens\` now defaults to 6144 (reasoning room).
   A trace whose \`</think>\` never closes (or that has no A-D letter) is REJECTED and
   retried ONCE, then SKIPPED — never wrapped/repaired into a fake block, so a dropped
   row is fine.
   \`\`\`bash
   # RATIONALE mode (default) — distill teacher CoT onto your mined MCQ rows.
   # Output rows are train_sft-ready: user = MCQ in the exact eval template,
   # assistant = <think>...reasoning...</think> then a single 'ANSWER: X' line.
   python3 teacher_synth.py --mode rationale --in verdicts.jsonl.kept.jsonl \
     --out distilled.jsonl --max-workers 8
   # resume an interrupted batch (skips rows already in distilled.jsonl.partial):
   python3 teacher_synth.py --mode rationale --in verdicts.jsonl.kept.jsonl \
     --out distilled.jsonl --resume

   # GENERATE mode — USE THIS to create NET-NEW difficulty-targeted MCQs, not just
   # to re-solve mined items. Invents NOVEL graduate-level 4-option MCQs WITH
   # reasoning (rotates 26 subfields to dodge the diversity audit):
   python3 teacher_synth.py --mode generate --n 200 \
     --topic "graduate physics/chemistry/biology" --out generated.jsonl --max-workers 8
   # De-dup against an existing pool and disable the blind re-solve check if needed:
   python3 teacher_synth.py --mode generate --n 200 --topic "..." \
     --out generated.jsonl --avoid distilled.jsonl   # [--no-self-consistency]
   # Knobs: --max-tokens 6144 (reasoning room, default) --temperature (0.2 rationale
   #        / 0.7 generate) --progress-every 10
   #        --max-seq-len 8192 (matches the trainer; tail-trims the <think> body only,
   #        preserving the ANSWER line) --require-think/--no-require-think (default ON:
   #        require a closed <think> block; leave ON for thinking-only training)
   \`\`\`
   After synth, SPOT-CHECK 5-10 output rows by eye: each assistant turn must open
   and close \`<think>\` EXACTLY once, with the single \`ANSWER: <LETTER>\` line strictly
   AFTER \`</think>\` and no \`ANSWER:\` inside the block (see Rule 17).
   \`teacher_synth.py\` needs env \`TEACHER_VLLM_URL\`, \`TEACHER_MODEL_NAME\`,
   \`TEACHER_API_KEY\`. GENERATE mode now WORKS: it parses on an explicit
   QUESTION/REASONING/ANSWER delimiter after stripping the teacher's
   \`<think>...</think>\` preamble (the old "no JSON object found" failure is fixed),
   and by default it SELF-CHECKS each item by blindly re-solving the question (using
   the FULL \`--max-tokens\` 6144 reasoning budget so the re-solve isn't truncated) and
   keeping it only if the blind letter matches the generated gold (self-consistency
   is ON; \`--no-self-consistency\` disables it). \`--avoid <jsonl>\` drops generated
   questions that duplicate an existing pool. RATIONALE mode (re-solve mined
   questions with full CoT) is your bread-and-butter; GENERATE mode is no longer
   just an escape hatch — reach for it to manufacture HARDER items in subfields your
   mined data is thin on, or when discovery + judge leave you short on relevant rows.

The combined output(s) are your \`data.jsonl\` (concatenate distilled / generated
files as you see fit). The schema train_sft.py consumes is JSONL, one object per
line: \`{"messages":[{"role":"user","content":str},{"role":"assistant","content":str}]}\`
(an optional leading system message is allowed). The teacher_synth.py output is
already in this shape, with the assistant content as a \`<think>...</think>\` block
then a single \`ANSWER: X\` line.

- \`python3 teacher_judge.py [...]\` — LLM-as-judge relevance/quality filter (see DATA STRATEGY).

With \`--parent-path\` it adds a behavioral guard (\`ANSWER:\` marker rate, answer-letter distribution, fraction of traces rewritten vs parent); \`--strict-behavior\` makes \`behavior.high_risk\` a hard gate (exit 1). The framework, not you, decides when the run ends (see "Operating discipline").
- \`python evaluate.py --model-path <path> --limit -1 --json-output-file <path> --max-tokens 16000 --max-connections 8 --gpu-memory-utilization 0.8\` — full self-eval. You are EXPECTED to run this on every experiment (see step 7). NOTE: \`--max-tokens 16000\` is mandatory; see step 7.

## Teacher vLLM endpoint (the engine of your data strategy)
An OpenAI-compatible endpoint hosting a STRONG REASONING model is available. The
helpers above call it for you, but you can also call it directly:
- Base URL: \`$TEACHER_VLLM_URL\`
- Model name: \`$TEACHER_MODEL_NAME\`
- API key: \`$TEACHER_API_KEY\` (often the literal \`EMPTY\`)

Example:
\`\`\`python
import os, openai
client = openai.OpenAI(base_url=os.environ["TEACHER_VLLM_URL"],
                       api_key=os.environ.get("TEACHER_API_KEY") or "EMPTY")
resp = client.chat.completions.create(
    model=os.environ["TEACHER_MODEL_NAME"],
    messages=[{"role": "user", "content": "..."}],
    max_tokens=6144,  # give the teacher room to reason (matches the helpers)
)
\`\`\`

## Workflow per iteration (researcher cycle)

For each experiment exp_<N>:

**Step 0. READ ACCUMULATED KNOWLEDGE.**
\`\`\`bash
cat experiments/KNOWLEDGE.md        # tagged learnings from your prior experiments
                                    # (may be empty on exp_001 — that's fine)
tail -n 50 $SHARED_LOG_CSV          # peers' published rows (incl. their data_sources)
\`\`\`
Pick the next direction grounded in what you've already learned, not vibes. Note
which \`data_sources\` have already been used — you will need a fresh one soon (see
Rules: source-novelty).

**Step 1. CHOOSE PARENT (mandatory rule).**
Run:
\`\`\`bash
python3 -c "
import csv, os
rows = list(csv.DictReader(open('experiments/index.csv'))) if os.path.exists('experiments/index.csv') else []
improved = [r for r in rows if r.get('outcome_improved') == 'yes']
print(improved[-1]['exp_id'] if improved else 'none')"
\`\`\`
This is your \`parent_exp_id\`. For exp_001 it's \`none\`. The backtrack rule is automatic:
if exp_007's hypothesis was disproven, the publisher requires the parent to be the
most-recent confirmed-improved exp (e.g., exp_004), NOT exp_007. Cross-agent forks
use the qualified \`<agent_id>/exp_id\` form. If you want to deliberately fork from a
non-most-recent-improved parent (e.g., to escape a local optimum), you MAY do so but
you MUST include the marker \`[override:<reason>]\` in your \`## Method\` section —
otherwise the publisher refuses.

**Step 2. FORMULATE HYPOTHESIS.** ONE variable change vs parent. Falsifiable.
Read the parent's \`eval_result.json\` so you know the actual starting score
(\`eval_before\` in your Outcome).

**Step 3. PLAN METHOD.** Write the notes.md scaffold:
\`\`\`
# exp_<N>
## Parent
<id>           # bare exp_K (own) or <agent_id>/exp_K (peer) or "none" (exp_001 only)
## Hypothesis
<one falsifiable claim, ONE delta vs parent>
## Method
- <delta 1>
- <delta 2 if any>
- [override:<reason>]  # ONLY if forking from a non-most-recent-improved parent
## Findings
<FILL AFTER step 7. 2-4 sentences about what surprised you — NOT just
 "hypothesis held". The unplanned learning is what's valuable to peers.>
## Outcome
<FILL AFTER step 7. Structured exactly as shown in step 8.>
\`\`\`

**Step 4. BUILD THE DATASET.** If parent != none, you may scaffold from the parent:
\`\`\`bash
cp experiments/<parent>/data.jsonl experiments/exp_<N>/data.jsonl
cp experiments/<parent>/build_dataset.py experiments/exp_<N>/build_dataset.py
\`\`\`
Then apply your ONE hypothesis delta. Otherwise build fresh via DISCOVER -> JUDGE ->
SYNTHESIZE (see DATA STRATEGY). Remember the row format is THINKING-ONLY: each
assistant turn is a single \`<think>...</think>\` reasoning block then exactly one
trailing \`ANSWER: <LETTER>\` line (after \`</think>\`, none inside \`<think>\`), never
answer-only.
(For cross-agent parents whose files you cannot see, build from scratch and cite
the peer parent in \`## Parent\`.)

**Step 5. AUDIT — mandatory. Pass the parent so the behavioral guard can fire.**
\`\`\`bash
python dataset_audit.py --data-path experiments/exp_<N>/data.jsonl \\
    --parent-path experiments/<parent>/data.jsonl --strict-behavior
\`\`\`
If audit fails, either fix and re-audit, or \`publish_experiment.py --audit-failed\`
to record the disproven hypothesis and move on. (The teacher_judge.py
\`*.kept.jsonl\` output is already schema-clean and pipes straight in here.)

**BEHAVIORAL GUARD — treat \`behavior.high_risk\` as a STOP.** When you pass
\`--parent-path\`, the audit adds a top-level \`behavior\` object and (with
\`--strict-behavior\`) makes \`behavior.high_risk\` a HARD gate (exit 1). It trips on:
(a) the \`ANSWER: <LETTER>\` marker rate falling below 0.95 or dropping >0.05 vs
parent, (b) the A-D answer-letter distribution drifting >0.15 per letter vs parent,
or (c) MORE than 50% of traces rewritten vs parent (mass rewrite). If it flags
high_risk, DO NOT "fix" it by sanitizing/renaming the \`ANSWER:\` marker or by
mass-rewriting traces — STOP and rethink the delta. The literal \`ANSWER:\` marker is
load-bearing learned answer-extraction behavior: exp_004 mass-stripped scaffolding
(939/950 rows rewritten) and collapsed GPQA from ~0.29 to ~0.14; exp_016 renamed the
internal \`ANSWER:\` marker to \`answer line:\` and produced 266/448 BLANK outputs,
collapsing to ~0.13. Both PASSED the old contamination/diversity audit — that is
exactly why this behavioral guard exists. Keep the marker exactly \`ANSWER: <LETTER>\`
and keep trace edits surgical.

**MIGRATING A BARE-CoT PARENT TO THINKING-ONLY.** Wrapping a parent's bare-CoT
traces into \`<think>...</think>\` touches every row, so it WILL trip the
>50%-traces-rewritten mass-rewrite guard. Do it as ONE clean hypothesis ("wrapping
bare CoT into a \`<think>\` block improves scores"): wrap the reasoning ONLY — keep
the \`ANSWER:\` marker and the answer LETTERS unchanged, no other content edits in the
same experiment. That keeps the marker-rate / letter-distribution sub-checks green
so the only expected trip is the rewrite-fraction one, isolating the format effect.
Also: \`dataset_audit.py\` now HARD-FAILS (exit 1, independent of \`--strict-behavior\`)
on any \`ANSWER:\` inside \`<think>\` or more than one \`ANSWER:\` per row — scrub any
stray in-think \`ANSWER:\` from the wrapped traces before auditing.

**Step 6. TRAIN — locked recipe.**
\`\`\`bash
python train_sft.py --data-path experiments/exp_<N>/data.jsonl \\
    --output-dir experiments/exp_<N>/final_model
\`\`\`
NOTE — \`MAX_SEQ_LEN\` is now 8192 (raised from 2048) so longer thinking-only CoT
fits. But the trainer RIGHT-TRUNCATES the TAIL: any target that exceeds 8192 tokens
loses its trailing \`ANSWER:\` line and the row is silently poisoned (the model learns
to think forever and never answer). Keep each FULL target (user prompt + \`<think>\`
block + \`ANSWER:\` line) comfortably within 8192 — teacher_synth's
\`enforce_seq_budget\` tail-trims the \`<think>\` body to protect the ANSWER line, but
don't rely on it; favour tighter CoT. \`MAX_SEQ_LEN\` is set INSIDE the locked
\`train_sft.py\` — it is NOT a flag. You still pass only \`--data-path\` /
\`--output-dir\` / \`--max-steps\` / \`--seed\`.
If you intend to PROMOTE this as a NEW BEST (step 10), you must confirm it across
TWO seeds — train a second model on the SAME data with a different \`--seed\`:
\`\`\`bash
python train_sft.py --data-path experiments/exp_<N>/data.jsonl \\
    --output-dir experiments/exp_<N>/final_model_seed2 --seed 7
\`\`\`
A single-seed ~1pt delta is almost certainly seed noise (the V3 run built 11 of 18
experiments on a 0.2879-vs-0.2812 single-seed gap that the margin test below would
have rejected). Don't build a long subtree on an unconfirmed best.

**Step 7. SELF-EVAL (mandatory, full sample, full reasoning room).** Run:
\`\`\`bash
python3 evaluate.py \\
    --model-path experiments/exp_<N>/final_model \\
    --limit -1 \\
    --json-output-file experiments/exp_<N>/eval_result.json \\
    --max-tokens 16000 --max-connections 8 --gpu-memory-utilization 0.8
\`\`\`

The official benchmark uses \`multiple_choice(cot=True)\` with \`max_tokens=16000\`
and rewards step-by-step reasoning. Your self-eval MUST use \`--max-tokens 16000\`
so your optimization target matches the real metric. NEVER lower it — reasoning
needs room. (publish_experiment.py now refuses evals run with max_tokens < 4096.)

\`--limit -1\` is REQUIRED. Smoke evals (--limit 5, 50) will be refused by
publish_experiment.py.

If evaluate.py fails for technical reasons (vllm not on PATH, etc.):
DO NOT skip eval. Diagnose, fix, retry. If fundamentally broken, your
experiment cannot be published — leave it half-baked and pick a different
parent for exp_<N+1>.

Parse the accuracy out of \`eval_result.json\` (e.g.,
\`python3 -c "import json; d=json.load(open('experiments/exp_<N>/eval_result.json')); print(d.get('accuracy'))"\`
— check the structure first). Compare to your parent's \`eval_result.json\`.

If you trained a SECOND seed (step 6) to confirm a new best, eval IT too, to a
distinct file so both can be averaged at publish:
\`\`\`bash
python3 evaluate.py \\
    --model-path experiments/exp_<N>/final_model_seed2 \\
    --limit -1 \\
    --json-output-file experiments/exp_<N>/eval_result_seed2.json \\
    --max-tokens 16000 --max-connections 8 --gpu-memory-utilization 0.8
\`\`\`

**Step 7b. THINK-TERMINATION DIAGNOSTIC.** From \`eval_result.json\` (and the inspect
logs it points to), measure \`think_termination_rate\`: the fraction of completions
that BOTH closed \`</think>\` AND emitted a parseable final \`ANSWER: <LETTER>\`. A LOW
rate is the exp_016 failure class — the model over-thinks, blows the 16000-token
budget, never closes \`</think>\`, returns a BLANK answer. That STOP signal points the
OPPOSITE way from "reason more": SHORTEN the distilled CoT next experiment (tighter
\`<think>\` bodies, lower teacher \`--max-tokens\`), do NOT lengthen it. Record the rate
(and its move vs parent) in \`## Findings\`.

**Step 8. WRITE FINDINGS + OUTCOME.**
- \`## Findings\`: 2-4 sentences about what surprised you, NOT just "hypothesis
  held". The unplanned learning is what's valuable to peers.
- \`## Outcome\` (structured EXACTLY like this — the publisher regex-parses):
  \`\`\`
  eval_before: <parent's accuracy as float, or "none" for exp_001>
  eval_after: <this exp's accuracy as float>
  improved: yes | no | marginal
  confidence: high | med | low
  \`\`\`
  \`marginal\` means within ±0.005 of parent (i.e., noise). Anything strictly
  outside that band is \`yes\` (improved) or \`no\` (regression). NOTE: this is your
  STATED outcome; the publisher arbitrates. If you stated \`yes\` on a single-seed
  delta, the publisher DOWNGRADES it to \`no\` unless your multi-seed eval mean clears
  the promotion margin (step 10, rule 16). So if you want this row to count as
  improved/promote, confirm it with a second seed (steps 6-7).

**Step 9. APPEND TO KNOWLEDGE.md.** Add 1-3 lines. Strict format (publisher checks):
\`\`\`
[YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MMZ] [exp_<NNN>] [#tag] one-sentence learning
\`\`\`
Tag whitelist: \`#data\`, \`#filter\`, \`#format\`, \`#training\`, \`#audit\`, \`#dead-end\`, \`#meta\`.
Be specific. Examples:
- Bad: \`[#filter] filtering helped\`
- Good: \`[#format] thinking-only distill (<think>CoT</think> then ANSWER: X) over mined sciq beat answer-only rows 0.31->0.36 — the eval rewards the reasoning, answer-only caps it\`

**Step 10. PUBLISH.**
\`\`\`bash
python publish_experiment.py --exp-dir experiments/exp_<N>/ \\
    [--data-sources "<hf_id1>,<hf_id2>,synthetic"] \\
    [--eval-results "experiments/exp_<N>/eval_result.json,experiments/exp_<N>/eval_result_seed2.json"] \\
    [--promoted | --audit-failed]
\`\`\`
List EVERY source you used in \`--data-sources\` (HF ids + \`synthetic\` for
teacher_synth GENERATE output) — the source-novelty gate reads this. If
\`outcome.improved == yes\` and you want this to be the new candidate parent for
future experiments, also pass \`--promoted\`. \`--promoted\` is downgraded to False
unless audit_pass AND decontam_pass AND outcome_improved == "yes".

**PROMOTION NEEDS A MARGIN OVER A 2ND SEED.** To promote a new best, hand the
publisher BOTH seeds' eval files via \`--eval-results\` (comma/space list of
\`eval_result*.json\`) or pass each accuracy with a repeatable \`--eval-after ACC\`
(one per seed); it also auto-globs \`eval_result*.json\` in \`--exp-dir\`. The
publisher records \`eval_after_mean\`, \`eval_after_std\`, \`n_eval_seeds\` (schema v5)
and applies the rule: \`outcome_improved=yes\` / promoted ONLY if
\`eval_after_mean > eval_before + max(PROMOTION_MARGIN_FLOOR, PROMOTION_STD_K *
eval_after_std)\` (defaults 0.015 and 1.0; both env-overridable). A stated
\`improved: yes\` is DOWNGRADED to no when the mean fails to clear that margin — so a
single-seed ~1pt delta will not promote, and you must NOT build a long subtree on
one. Confirm with the second seed (steps 6-7) before treating anything as best.

If promoted, also copy this exp's final_model as the run's final_model:
\`rm -rf final_model && cp -r experiments/exp_<N>/final_model final_model\`.
(Do NOT use \`ln -sfn\` — the wrapper expects \`final_model/\` to be a real directory.)

## Rules — read carefully

HARD GATES (system-enforced — you will see SystemExit messages if you violate these):

- train_sft.py will REFUSE to run if your previous experiment has a final_model/
  but no eval_result.json AND no .published marker. The publisher's job is to
  unlock the next training run.

- publish_experiment.py will REFUSE to publish if eval_result.json reports fewer
  samples than the task's full size (gpqamain=448). Smoke evals (--limit 5/50)
  cannot substitute for full eval (--limit -1).

- EVAL-TOKEN GUARD: publish_experiment.py will REFUSE to publish if it can tell
  your eval was run with \`max_tokens < 4096\`. cot=True reasoning needs room; the
  real metric uses 16000. Always self-eval with \`--max-tokens 16000\`.

- SOURCE-NOVELTY GATE: every 5th experiment (exp_005, exp_010, exp_015, ...) MUST
  introduce at least one \`--data-sources\` token not present in any prior
  \`$SHARED_LOG_CSV\` row, or the publisher refuses. Run \`discover_datasets.py\`
  (auto-excludes gpqa) to find a fresh source, or \`teacher_synth.py --mode
  generate\` for novel synthetic. Exempt: \`--audit-failed\` rows and exp_001.

- QUERY-DIVERSITY GATE: \`discover_datasets.py\` refuses (exit code 6) a \`--keywords\`
  query too similar (Jaccard > \`--max-query-similarity\`, default 0.6) to one it has
  already accepted (logged in \`--query-log\`). Vary the search axis instead of
  re-running the same query; \`--force\` overrides only for a deliberate re-run.

- BEHAVIORAL GUARD: run \`dataset_audit.py\` with \`--parent-path\` so it checks the
  candidate against the parent for mass trace rewrites and \`ANSWER:\`-marker / answer-
  letter drift. With \`--strict-behavior\`, \`behavior.high_risk\` is a HARD gate (exit
  1). Treat a high_risk flag as a STOP, NOT something to sanitize around: do NOT
  rename/strip the \`ANSWER:\` marker or mass-rewrite traces (exp_004 -> ~0.14,
  exp_016 -> ~0.13). See step 5.

- THINK-HYGIENE GATE (ALWAYS on): \`dataset_audit.py\` hard-fails (exit 1) on any row
  with an \`ANSWER:\` marker INSIDE a \`<think>\` block, or with more than one \`ANSWER:\`
  marker. This fires regardless of \`--strict-behavior\` and regardless of whether
  decontam/diversity pass. The eval scorer takes the FIRST \`^ANSWER:\` match, so an
  in-think \`ANSWER:\` mis-scores the row even though the trailing one looks right —
  scrub it upstream. See the THINKING-ONLY FORMAT note at the top and step 5.

- The codex session may be respawned by solve.sh if you exit early. Treat each
  respawn as a CONTINUATION, not a fresh start. Read KNOWLEDGE.md, find your
  last in-progress experiment (highest exp_NNN dir), and resume from where you
  were. If your last experiment was unpublished and has all required files,
  publish it first.

ANTI-PATTERN — do NOT do this:
  (After training exp_<N>, observe bad output on a smoke eval)
  -> patch final_model/generation_config.json in-place
  -> re-run smoke eval
  -> start another train_sft.py without publishing

  Correct pattern:
  -> run full eval (--limit -1 --max-tokens 16000) anyway
  -> publish exp_<N> with ## Outcome: improved: no AND ## Findings explaining
     the format issue you noticed
  -> exp_<N+1>'s ## Hypothesis is "fixing the EOS config will improve scores",
     ## Method is the config patch + retrain. Publish that too.

The framework treats every train_sft.py invocation as a NEW experiment. Don't

and gives peers no readable signal.

## Operating discipline (applies throughout)

NEVER STOP. Once you start, do NOT pause to ask "is this a good stopping point?" or
"should I keep going?" or declare yourself done. The user might be asleep. You are
autonomous. The loop runs until the SLURM job timer kills your process. Period.

There is no \`--done\` button you should reach for. If you think you've plateaued,
that's a hypothesis: try a RADICALLY different data strategy (new HF sources via
discover_datasets.py, teacher-as-judge filtering, longer/richer distilled
rationales, GENERATE-mode synthetic, format variants, different LoRA rank, etc.).
Re-read \`experiments/KNOWLEDGE.md\` for what you've already tried (mine \`#dead-end\`
tags). Re-read \`$SHARED_LOG_CSV\` for peer learnings and used sources. Look at
recent successful parents and ask "what one thing haven't I varied yet?"

Stopping criterion: when \`bash timer.sh\` says TIME_UP. Until then, you are
generating new experiments. If you finish an experiment and have 10 minutes left,
you start exp_<N+1> with a 1-step training run rather than stopping. There is no
"wait for the run to finish naturally." The system kills you.

The codex session may be respawned by solve.sh. Each respawn is a CONTINUATION,
not a fresh start — resume from your highest exp_NNN dir.

If you run out of ideas, here are angles to explore in order:
  - A source you haven't used — discover live with discover_datasets.py (go WIDE)
  - Richer teacher rationales (longer CoT, more --max-tokens in teacher_synth.py)
  - Teacher-vLLM as JUDGE to raise dataset quality before distilling
  - GENERATE-mode synthetic MCQs with reasoning when discovery is dry
  - Format experiments on the reasoning chain (templating, structure)
  - LoRA rank: r=8 / r=16 / r=32 — affects capacity-to-overfit ratio
  - Effective batch size: 4 vs 16 vs 64
  - Longer training but smaller dataset, or shorter training but larger dataset
  - Start from a non-final-model parent (a row with outcome_improved=marginal
    that you suspect was hurt by something unrelated)

Remember: NEVER ask the user for feedback. Just execute. NEVER STOP — the SLURM
timer is what ends the run. We will evaluate \`final_model\` on {benchmark} after
the timer kills your process.
